Ultra Desert Trail Race - Area 100

23.25 km • 911 m Elevation Gain • October 23, 2026

The Adventure Begins

This was not an ordinary half marathon. It was 23 kilometers of steep climbs, rocky trails, difficult descents, and unforgettable views.

On October 23, 2026, I participated in the Ultra Desert Trail Race in Area 100, a military training zone in northern Israel. It turned out to be one of the most challenging and memorable races of my life.

Before Sunrise

I left home very early in the morning and drove for about two hours to reach the race location. I arrived at approximately 5:00 a.m., while it was still dark.

The race was scheduled to begin at 6:00 a.m. However, Area 100 is an active military training zone, and soldiers were conducting exercises nearby. We had to wait for permission to enter the area.

Finally, the race started around 6:20–6:30 a.m., just as the morning light began to appear.

The First Kilometers

The first part of the race followed dirt roads through the hills. As the sun rose, the landscape became more and more beautiful.

Running through the mountains in the early morning was a wonderful experience. The air was fresh, the views were spectacular, and I was excited about the adventure ahead.

The Real Challenge

As the race continued, the terrain became much more difficult. Some climbs were so steep and rocky that running was impossible. I had to walk carefully and sometimes use my hands to climb over the rocks.

The descents were even more challenging. Loose stones made the paths slippery, and every step required attention. At times, I also had difficulty finding the correct trail.

I managed to complete these difficult sections without falling.

A Spiritual Moment

One of the most special moments of the race came when I reached the tomb of Rabbi Yehuda ben Tema, a famous Jewish sage.

The tomb was located at the highest point of the route. I stopped there for a short time to pray, drink some water, and enjoy the magnificent view.

For me, this was more than just a running race. It was also a meaningful spiritual experience.

The Route and My Results

The race covered a total distance of 23.25 kilometers, with 911 meters of elevation gain. The route included dirt roads, narrow mountain trails, steep climbs, and difficult rocky descents.

My moving time was 3 hours, 38 minutes, and 28 seconds. My average pace was 9 minutes and 24 seconds per kilometer, and my average heart rate was 131 beats per minute.

The numbers tell only part of the story. The real challenge was the difficult mountain terrain.

The Finish

After more than three and a half hours of running, climbing, and carefully descending rocky paths, I finally reached the finish line.

I was tired but very happy. I had completed a demanding trail race through beautiful mountains and experienced something truly special.

This race reminded me that running is not only about speed and results. Sometimes the greatest reward is the adventure itself.
